Introduction

Parking Studies help determine how much parking is available, how it is being used, and whether existing parking facilities are sufficient to meet demand.

This section introduces fundamental parking survey methods, parking performance indicators, on-street parking configurations, and parking requirements used in planning and development studies.

Parking Bays and Occupancy

A parking study evaluates the characteristics of parking within a specific area, facility, street, or development. It provides information about parking supply, demand, duration, turnover, and utilization.

Parking Accumulation

Defined as the number of vehicles parked at a given instant of time. This is normally expressed by accumulation curve.

Accumulation Curve

Graph obtained by plotting the number of bays occupied with respect to time.

Parking Volume

Total number of vehicles parked at a given duration of time. Actual volume of vehicles entered in the study area is recorded but this does not account for the repetition of vehicles

Parking Load

Parking Load represents the total parking activity or parking demand accumulated over the survey period. Usually expressed in vehicle-hours.

Average Parking Duration

The length of time a vehicle remains parked. It is also the ratio of total vehicle hours to the number of vehicles parked.

\[\text{Parking Duration} =\frac{\text{Parking Load}}{\text{Parking Volume}}\]

Parking Turnover

The number of vehicles using a parking space during a specified period. Ratio of number of vehicles parked in a duration to the number of parking bays available

\[\text{Parking Turnover} =\frac{\text{Parking Volume}}{\text{Number of Bays Available}}\]

Parking Index

Indicates the level of utilization of available parking spaces.Ratio of number of bays occupied in a time duration to the total space available.

\[\text{Parking Index} =\frac{\text{Parking Load}}{\text{Parking Capacity}} \times 100\]

Sample Problem

A parking study was conducted in a small parking area having 4 parking bays for a period of 2.5 hours (150 minutes). The occupancy of each bay is shown in the figure.

Determine the following:

  • Parking Volume
  • Parking Load (in vehicle-hours)
  • Average Parking Duration (in minutes)
  • Parking Turnover
  • Parking Index

Solution:


Parking Volume

Since there are 6 distinct vehicles, thus, parking volume is 6.

Parking Load

Number of vehicles times the time interval

\[ PL = (1+2+3+3+2+2+3+3+4+3+2)\left(\frac{15}{60}\right) \]

\[\text{Parking Load} = 7\ \text{vehicle-hours}\]

Average Parking Duration

\[ \text{Ave. Parking Duration} = \frac{\text{Parking Load}}{\text{Parking Volume}} \]

\[ \text{Ave. Parking Duration} = \frac{7\ \text{veh-hr}}{6\ \text{veh}} \]

\[ \text{Ave. Parking Duration}=70\ \text{minutes}\]

Parking Turnover

\[ \text{Parking Turnover} = \frac{\text{Parking Volume}} {\text{No. of Bays} \times \text{Study Duration}} \]

\[ \text{Parking Turnover} = \frac{6\ \text{veh}} {4\ \text{bays} \times 2.5\ \text{hr}} \]

\[ \text{Parking Turnover} = 0.60\ \text{veh/hr/bay} \]

Parking Index

\[ \text{Parking Index} = \frac{\text{Parking Load}}{\text{Parking Capacity}} \times 100 \]

\[ \text{Parking Index} = \frac{7\ \text{veh-hr}} {4\ \text{bays} \times 2.5\ \text{hr}} \times 100 \]

\[ \text{Parking Index} = 70\% \]

In and Out Parking Survey

An In-and-Out Survey records vehicles entering and leaving a parking facility during the survey period.

This method is useful for determining parking accumulation, parking demand, and turnover.

Basic Data Collected:

  • Time of Entry
  • Time of Exit
  • Number of Vehicles Entering
  • Number of Vehicles Leaving
  • Parking Accumulation

Typical Procedure:

  • Establish the survey period.
  • Record the number of vehicles entering the parking facility.
  • Record the number of vehicles leaving.
  • Determine the number of vehicles parked during each time interval.
  • Calculate parking accumulation and other parking indicators.

Sample Problem:

In-Out Survey is conducted for one hour for a parking area with 60 bays. The initial count of cars parked was 34. The survey yielded the following information.

Solution:

The number of vehicles parked at a particular time can be determined using:

\[\text{Accumulation}=\text{Previous Accumulation}+\text{Vehicles In}-\text{Vehicles Out}\]

Parking Load is computed as:

\[\text{Parking Load}=(\text{Accumulation})(\text{Time Interval})\]

\[\text{Parking Load for 0-15 mins}=(31\ \text{veh})\left(\frac{15\ \text{mins}}{60\ \text{mins}}\right)\]

The total parking load for the entire duration is 31 veh-hrs.


The Parking Index is computed as:

\[ \text{Parking Index} = \frac{\text{Parking Load}} {\text{Total Time Interval}} \times 100 \]

However, this does not provide the data regarding the time duration for which a particular vehicle parked, therefore, it cannot be used to define or to decide the parking fee.

Parking Patrol Survey

A Parking Patrol Survey, also known as a parking occupancy survey, involves repeatedly observing parking spaces along a street or within a parking area.

The surveyor records whether each parking space is occupied or vacant at predetermined time intervals.

A surveyor also known as patroller walks or drives through the study area at regular time intervals and records the license plate number, location, arrival time, and duration.

Different types of Parking

Parking design determines how vehicles are positioned within the available roadway width.

Common parking configurations include:

a. Right-angle Parking or Perpendicular Parking

b. Parallel Parking

c. 30° Parking

d. 45° Parking

e. 60° Parking

Perpendicular Parking

  • Vehicles are parked perpendicular to the direction of the road.
  • Consumes maximum width, curb length required is very little.
  • Accommodates maximum number of vehicles.
  • Requires greater maneuvering space.
  • May have greater potential for conflicts with moving vehicles.

\[L = 2.5N\]

Parallel Parking

  • Vehicles are parked parallel to the curb.
  • Consumes the maximum curb length but requires relatively little roadway width.
  • Generally, accommodates fewer vehicles per unit of curb length.
  • Least obstruction to the on-going traffic.

\[L = \frac{N}{6}\]

Diagonal Parking

  • \[\sin\theta = \frac{2.5}{x}\]
  • \[\cos(90^\circ-\theta) = \frac{x_1}{2.5}\]
  • \[\sin\theta = \frac{x_1}{2.5}\]
  • \[x_1 = 2.5\sin\theta\]
  • \[N = \frac{L-x_1}{x}\]
  • \[N = \frac{L-2.5\sin\theta}{2.5\csc\theta}\]

30 Degrees Parking

  • Vehicles are positioned at 30 degreees relative to the curb.
  • More vehicles can be parked compared to parallel parking but requires more roadway width.

\[N= \frac{L - 1.25}{5}\]

45 Degrees Parking

  • Vehicles are positioned at 45 degrees relative to the curb.
  • Provides convenient entry and exit.
  • Requires moderate roadway width.
  • Often provides a good balance between parking capacity and maneuverability.

\[N = \frac{L - 1.768}{3.536}\]

60 Degrees Parking

  • Vehicles are positioned at 60 degrees relative to the curb.
  • Provides relatively high parking capacity.
  • Requires greater roadway width.
  • Requires careful consideration of vehicle maneuvering and traffic circulation.

\[N = \frac{L - 2.165}{2.887}\]

Swept Path Analysis

AutoTURN is a vehicle-path simulation software used to evaluate whether vehicles can safely and efficiently maneuver within a parking lot. In a commercial parking lot, the proposed layout (including parking spaces, drive aisles, entrances/exits, and circulation routes) is tested using the turning paths of representative design vehicles such as cars, delivery trucks, buses, or other vehicles.

Test Vehicle

The profile of the test vehicle used to assess the road geometry of the development's parking area is shown.

The Toyota Fortuner is used as a design vehicle in the AutoTURN simulation because its dimensions and turning characteristics provide a practical representation of a typical passenger vehicle/SUV that may use a commercial drive-thru and parking facility.

Its vehicle width, track width, wheelbase, and steering-lock characteristics allow AutoTURN to generate realistic swept paths and turning envelopes.

Parking Requirements

Parking requirements determine the minimum number of parking spaces that may be required for a particular land use or development.

National Building Code (NBC)

The National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096) and its implementing rules provide provisions relevant to building development, including parking and loading considerations.

Parking requirements should be checked against the applicable NBC provisions and current implementing rules for the specific building occupancy and development type.
